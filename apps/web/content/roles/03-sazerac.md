Process engineering at the Barton 1792 Distillery in Bardstown, a very
old plant, with a small share of the work at Sazerac's Glenmore
facility in Owensboro. Reported to the plant engineer. The work was
improving processes and equipment in a distillery that had to keep
running while it was being improved.

Replaced the dry house, the largest project of the role. The original
was built in 1948 and was still in service, with availability,
performance and quality all badly degraded. Built its replacement and
took dry house OEE (overall equipment effectiveness) from between 0.40
and 0.59 to above 0.90.

The plant ran on Allen-Bradley, Siemens and GE PLCs, mostly
Allen-Bradley, supervised through Schneider Electric's Citect SCADA.
Wrote updated HMI screens and trending tools for that system. Drives,
motor starters and instruments came from six or seven equipment makers,
so standardized on Allen-Bradley PowerFlex 525 and 700 series drives
and Emerson instrumentation, replacing nonstandard equipment as it
failed rather than all at once.

Data collection barely existed at the start, and what did exist sat in
silos. Began standardizing how plant data was collected and built new
relational and time-series databases to hold it.

Owned the operational technology side of the company's ISO 27001
program: connectivity, data collection and calibration of plant
instrumentation, and network security of the OT networks.

Regulated manufacturing throughout, under TTB rules, Distilled Spirits
Council (DISCUS) standards, NFPA codes and the National Electrical Code.
